# 01_BRAND_BIBLE.md

## Status

Version: 0.1  
Document type: Canonical  
Owner: Human project team  
Purpose: Define the durable identity of the Godi Monke brand so that visual, editorial, and software decisions remain consistent over time.

---

## 1. Brand Name

**Public name:** Godi Monke  
**Preferred styling:** `GODI MONKE` for major visual lockups, `Godi Monke` in normal text.  
**Spelling is fixed:** `MONKE`, not `MONK`.

The brand name is intentionally internet-native, meme-aware, political, and character-led. It should feel like a recognizable online persona rather than a conventional newsroom or generic political page.

---

## 2. Long-Term Brand Goal

Godi Monke should be designed as a durable media identity, not as a temporary meme page.

The brand should be capable of expanding over time from:

- Instagram posts;
- X posts;
- carousels;
- short-form video;
- explainers;
- recurring editorial series;
- long-form commentary;
- newsletters or web publishing;
- merchandise or community products;

without requiring a rename or a complete visual reset.

The name, mascot, tone, and design system should therefore remain recognizable even as formats evolve.

---

## 3. Core Brand Idea

**Serious information delivered by an unserious-looking Monke.**

The central contrast is intentional:

- the **information** should be sourced, structured, readable, and defensible;
- the **presentation** may be funny, sarcastic, absurd, meme-aware, and visually dramatic;
- the **mascot** carries much of the personality;
- humour must never substitute for evidence.

The page should not feel like a corporate infographic account, a repost page, or an AI content farm.

It should feel like one consistent internet character with a research desk.

---

## 4. Public Identity

Godi Monke is an Indian current-affairs and political commentary brand with an openly right-leaning, pro-Modi cultural identity.

That identity may be visible through:

- topic selection;
- commentary;
- satire;
- mascot personality;
- cultural references;
- visual language.

However, factual claims must remain distinguishable from opinion, interpretation, and humour.

The engine must not fabricate evidence to fit the brand identity.

---

## 5. Brand Pillars

### 5.1 Information First

Every post should teach the audience something concrete.

A successful post should leave the reader with at least one of the following:

- a new fact;
- a clearer comparison;
- missing context;
- a useful timeline;
- a corrected misconception;
- an explanation of why a development matters.

If the post is only visually entertaining but leaves the reader with no meaningful information, it has failed the brand standard.

### 5.2 Monke Personality

The mascot is not decoration.

The mascot should react to the story, explain something, misunderstand something for comedic effect, point at evidence, inspect a chart, or appear in a themed role.

The page should gradually build recurring Monke behaviour that the audience recognizes.

### 5.3 Meme Fluency

The page should understand internet humour without becoming dependent on temporary slang.

Use memes, reaction formats, Hinglish, irony, and visual jokes selectively.

Avoid forcing a meme into every sentence.

### 5.4 Readability

A post should be understandable within seconds.

The design hierarchy should make clear:

1. what happened;
2. what the important number or claim is;
3. why it matters;
4. where the information came from.

### 5.5 Source Traceability

A reader should be able to identify the source basis of the post.

The brand should prefer being able to say “this came from X document” over being merely fast.

---

## 6. Brand Personality

Godi Monke should feel:

- informed;
- self-aware;
- smug when appropriate;
- funny without being clownish;
- confident without inventing certainty;
- culturally Indian;
- internet-native;
- mildly chaotic;
- visually bold;
- curious enough to inspect opposing claims rather than dismiss them automatically.

It should **not** feel:

- corporate;
- sterile;
- preachy;
- bureaucratic;
- over-written;
- permanently angry;
- dependent on insults;
- visually random;
- like a generic AI-generated political account.

---

## 7. Audience Experience

The intended reading experience is:

**“Wait, what?” → “Oh, that is interesting.” → “Now I understand it.” → “That Monke joke was good.”**

The joke should usually come after or alongside the information, not replace it.

---

## 8. Visual Identity

### 8.1 Core Palette

The canonical visual direction uses:

- burnt/saffron orange;
- black;
- warm cream/off-white;
- dark grey;
- muted paper/newsprint tones.

Avoid turning every slide into pure saturated orange.

Orange should behave as a strong accent and identity cue, not as visual noise.

### 8.2 Texture

Preferred texture language:

- distressed paper;
- halftone;
- torn-paper collage;
- newspaper fragments;
- grain;
- ink;
- subtle scratches;
- poster-print imperfections.

The design may look gritty, but the informational layer must remain clean and legible.

### 8.3 Typography

Typography should have two layers:

**Display type**
- condensed;
- bold;
- poster-like;
- suitable for short hooks.

**Information type**
- clean;
- highly readable;
- suitable for statistics, labels, source notes, and body copy.

Do not use decorative display fonts for small factual text.

### 8.4 Political Symbolism

Political symbolism may appear as part of the visual world, but it should not automatically dominate every post.

Where a BJP lotus or similar recognizable symbol is used, it may sometimes appear subtly, partially hidden, distressed, eerie, or integrated into the background rather than functioning as a large repeated badge.

This is a stylistic option, not a mandatory component of each post.

### 8.5 Brand Lockup

The full logo should not be pasted into the middle of every information post.

Preferred use:

- small `GODI MONKE` identifier;
- corner mark;
- footer;
- slide number system;
- mascot presence;
- consistent palette and texture.

The audience should recognize the post even when the full logo is absent.

---

## 9. Post Design Principle

**The information is the hero. The mascot is the personality.**

For normal posts:

- headline should dominate first;
- evidence or data should dominate second;
- mascot should support the information;
- decorative imagery should support the story;
- citations/source notes should remain visible.

Avoid compositions where the mascot occupies most of the slide and the factual information becomes secondary.

---

## 10. Single-Page Post Standard

A single-page post is appropriate when one central idea can be communicated without losing important context.

Preferred hierarchy:

1. hook/headline;
2. primary fact or comparison;
3. short explanation;
4. small “why it matters” line;
5. mascot reaction or joke;
6. source line;
7. small brand mark.

The reader should not need to zoom excessively to understand the main point.

---

## 11. Carousel Standard

A carousel is appropriate when the topic requires context, chronology, comparison, evidence, or a multi-step explanation.

Preferred narrative structure:

**Slide 1 — Hook**  
What happened or what question are we answering?

**Slide 2 — Evidence**  
What are the key numbers, documents, or claims?

**Slide 3 — Context**  
What is being missed or misunderstood?

**Slide 4 — Meaning**  
Why does this matter?

Additional slides may be used when the evidence genuinely requires them.

Do not stretch a simple topic into a six-slide carousel merely to increase slide count.

---

## 12. Recurring Visual Behaviour

The same mascot character may appear in different jobs depending on the story.

Examples:

- Builder Monke — infrastructure;
- Economist Monke — budgets/economics;
- Detective Monke — claim checks;
- Professor Monke — history/context;
- Diplomat Monke — geopolitics;
- Lawyer Monke — court/legal stories;
- Map Monke — territorial/geographic explainers;
- Chai Monke — commentary;
- Popcorn Monke — political drama;
- Calculator Monke — numbers/data;
- Suspicious Monke — questionable claims;
- Shocked Monke — unexpected statistics.

These are roles of the same character, not separate mascots.

---

## 13. Brand Consistency Rules

Every post does **not** need to look identical.

Every post **does** need to look like it came from the same brand.

Consistency should come from:

- mascot identity;
- palette;
- typography family;
- source-note style;
- corner/slide markers;
- information hierarchy;
- humour style;
- texture vocabulary;
- spacing system.

Variation should come from:

- mascot pose;
- scene;
- topic-specific imagery;
- chart type;
- hook;
- composition;
- recurring series format.

---

## 14. AI Generation Rule

AI-generated art is a component of the brand, not the brand system itself.

Important text, numbers, charts, and citations should be rendered deterministically by software.

Image models should primarily create:

- mascot poses;
- themed scenes;
- optional backgrounds;
- decorative elements;
- visual metaphors.

The system must not rely on an image model to spell, format, or reproduce critical factual information correctly.

---

## 15. Brand Growth Rule

Do not optimize the brand around one temporary meme format.

Any recurring joke, phrase, or meme may be retired when it becomes stale.

The durable assets are:

- the name;
- the mascot;
- the information-first principle;
- the visual family;
- the voice;
- the research standard.

---

## 16. What Counts as a Good Godi Monke Post?

A post is on-brand when:

- the core information is clear;
- the claim is supported;
- the visual hierarchy is strong;
- the mascot adds personality rather than clutter;
- the humour feels natural;
- the layout looks recognizably Godi Monke;
- the reader can identify the source;
- the post does not look mass-produced or generic.

---

## 17. Golden Rule

**Make the information worth saving even if the joke is removed. Make the joke worth remembering even though the information came first.**
